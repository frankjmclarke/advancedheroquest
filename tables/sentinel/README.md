# Sentinel V

An Advanced HeroQuest campaign in five decks, played with the miniatures from
Milton Bradley's **Space Crusade** (1990) and its two expansions, **Eldar
Attack** (1991) and **Mission Dreadnought** (1991). The adventurers are Space
Marines. The last deck is held by the Eldar, and by nothing else.

Five quests appear in *Options → Load Tables*:

| Quest | Deck | Held by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sentinel1.tab` | 1 — Docking Spires | Gretchin and Orks |
| `sentinel2.tab` | 2 — Cargo Holds | Orks, Nobz, Chaos Androids |
| `sentinel3.tab` | 3 — Reactor Sumps | Genestealers, Androids, a Dreadnought |
| `sentinel4.tab` | 4 — Command Spire | Chaos Space Marines and Dreadnoughts |
| `sentinel5.tab` | 5 — The Sentinel Core | **Eldar only** |

Generate one map per deck and play them in order. A deck ends at a Drop Shaft
Down; the Core ends at the webway portal.

---

## The mission

Sentinel V is the last of the Adeptus Mechanicus watch-stations strung along
the Cadmus Reach, sunk into a dead moon and left to listen. For four hundred
years it did. Six weeks ago it stopped in the middle of a routine sweep, and
the sweep it was halfway through is still the last thing it transmitted.

A strike force teleports onto the upper spires. What they find on the way down
is a sequence, not a siege: an Ork scavenger mob in the mooring clamps, its
Nobz deeper in with Chaos Androids they did not build; the garrison gone into
the coolant sumps and come back as a Genestealer brood; and on the command
deck a warband of the Silent Hand, who opened the station and are the reason
any of the rest got in.

None of them are why the Eldar are here. At the bottom of the shaft a warhost
holds the Sentinel Core, and a Farseer is finishing something at the heart of
the machine that was begun a long time before the Imperium put a station on
this moon. They have opened a webway portal in the Core chamber. It is the
objective, and it is also the only way off Sentinel V.

---

## Rewards

Sentinel V pays in **Points**, the scoring unit of Space Crusade: what a squad
accumulates decides its promotions between missions. Every enemy is worth
Points, and so is anything carried off the deck. Read "Points" wherever
Advanced HeroQuest says gold.

## Rosters and profiles

Profiles use the notation the other quests in `tables\` use: Sp (Speed), WS
(Weapon Skill), BS (Ballistic Skill), S (Strength), T (Toughness), W (Wounds),
DD (Damage Dice), F (Fate).

### On the Eldar Leader

Eldar Attack supplies **one** leader miniature and a deck of ability cards.
Deck 5 does the same: the `Eldar-Leader` table rolls a named character —
Warlock Aedryn, Exarch Ythrannor of the Shrouded Blade, Spiritseer Nuadhu,
Exarch Maeliss the Patient, Autarch Kaelyth Duilan, Farseer Ulvaneth of the
Sentinel Host — and every one of them is that same figure, on the table for a
different reason and worth a different number of Points. Farseer Ulvaneth is
what the strike force came down here for; when the roll produces Ulvaneth in
an objective room, that is the room.

### On the Wraithlord

The Wraithlord entries in `core.tab` are a **proxy**: the Space Crusade
Dreadnought miniature, repainted in wraithbone, standing in for an Eldar
Wraithlord. It is an Eldar enemy, so Deck 5 stays all-Eldar either way, but it
is the one model on that deck that did not come out of the Eldar Attack box.
If you would rather not proxy, edit `core.tab` and point those rows at
`Eldar-Las-Cannon()` instead — the deck plays perfectly well without it.

## The files

```
tables\
    sentinel1.tab .. sentinel5.tab      the five quests
    sentinel\
        room1.tab       layout, decks 1-2 (open, fewer defended rooms)
        room2.tab       layout, decks 3-4 (cramped, more patrols, trapped)
        room3.tab       layout, deck 5   (held ground, portal instead of shaft)
        fittings.tab    Room-Furnish: the station's furniture
        hazard.tab      Hazards-Matrix: breaches, sumps, servitors, caskets
        equip.tab       Hidden-Treasure and Supply-Crate: salvage and wargear
        trap.tab        Room-Trap and Chest-Trap: station security, still armed
        monster.tab     one table per miniature, and the wargear it carries
        spires.tab      deck 1 warband
        holds.tab       deck 2 warband
        reactor.tab     deck 3 warband
        command.tab     deck 4 warband
        core.tab        deck 5 warband - Eldar only
```

Nothing here includes `standard\`. Those files are written for Warhammer:
gold crowns, magic swords, spell scrolls. Sentinel V replaces all four with
its own, so no fantasy vocabulary reaches the monster list.

Each warband file defines the same five tables — `Wandering-Monsters`,
`Lairs-Matrix`, `Quest-Rooms-Matrix`, `Specialist-Xenos`,
`Specialist-Xenos-Double` — plus `Lurker`, which is what is found waiting in a
maintenance crawl. Only one warband file is loaded at a time, so to write a
sixth deck, copy one of them, rewrite the five tables, and add a quest file in
`tables\` that includes it.
