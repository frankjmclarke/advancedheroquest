Below is a GM-facing, spoiler-heavy summary of all ten adventures in the uploaded Book of Adventures. I’ve kept the emphasis on premise, structure, objectives, major threats, and what makes each scenario distinctive rather than reproducing room-by-room instructions. The volume identifies these ten adventures as the complete quest collection.

1. The Shattered Amulet

The Shattered Amulet is the introductory campaign of the collection and is explicitly intended for beginning Heroes. Its background reaches back to an ancient war between Solkan the Avenger and the forces of Khorne. During Solkan’s duel with the monstrous general Fernadrang, a blow struck the magical Amulet Solkan wore and shattered it into four pieces. Those fragments were scattered, and in the present day the Skaven have begun hunting them. The last journal entry of Jervais the Revered reveals both the danger of the Skaven interest and the possibility that the Amulet has some importance in opposing the Daemon-General Praznagar.

The campaign sends the Heroes through four separate Skaven dungeons in a fixed order: The Warlord’s Lair, The Magic Maze, The Plague Temple, and The Amber Room. Each contains one fragment of the Amulet. The first two levels of each dungeon are largely generated using the standard exploration system; the first Quest Room on each level provides the route deeper. The third level is a specifically mapped Quest Location with scripted encounters, traps, secrets, and treasure. This creates a deliberate progression from open-ended exploration into a more tightly designed finale for each fragment.

Each of the four sites has its own identity. The Warlord’s Lair belongs to Clan Rictus and begins with a puzzle involving a Jade College symbol and matching talisman that lowers a concealed raised passage. Later dungeons introduce increasingly dangerous Skaven characters and specialists: an Eshin Assassin becomes available during the Magic Maze, Pestilens characters appear in the Plague Temple, and powerful Mors and Skryre leaders—including a Warlord, Warpweaver and White Skaven Sorcerer—can appear by the Amber Room.

The campaign’s real twist comes only after all four pieces are recovered and joined. The completed Amulet is not simply a protective relic. It was created by Solkan as a means of travelling to Solkan’s Prison of Ice, where Praznagar was imprisoned in frozen pure water after an earlier war with the Horned Rat. The Skaven were seeking the fragments because they understood what the Amulet could access. Once the Heroes reconstruct it, they are drawn into the unexpected fifth stage of the campaign. Thus what initially appears to be four related treasure hunts becomes a larger struggle involving the containment of an ancient Daemonic threat.

As a campaign, The Shattered Amulet introduces most of Advanced Heroquest’s core rhythms: repeated expeditions, random upper dungeon levels, mapped lower Quest Locations, accumulating special enemies, and a final revelation that recontextualizes the apparent objective.

2. The Quest for the Lichemaster

This campaign begins in Felspraag, north of the Grey Mountains. The Heroes are welcomed by Baron Gunther von Mecklenberg and his advisers, who explain that the region faces a growing Undead threat. Their enemy is the legendary Lichemaster, a necromantic power believed to be gathering strength again in a hidden mountain tomb. His servants have already begun to raid settlements, and the Baron’s conventional forces are increasingly occupied simply keeping the countryside from collapsing.

The key to defeating him lies in the Legend of the Four. In an earlier confrontation, four Heroes—Warrior, Wizard, Elf and Dwarf—used four legendary magical weapons against the Lichemaster. They nearly destroyed him, but at the instant of defeat he escaped in a catastrophic magical blast. The four Heroes disappeared and their weapons were scattered. Those relics are the Sword of the Flaming Heart, Wand of Conflagration, Power Bow of Loren, and World’s Edge Hammer. The new Heroes must recover those weapons before confronting the Lichemaster again.

The campaign is therefore structured as a succession of linked searches. Each recovered item supplies both additional power and clues leading toward the next. The first mission sends the Heroes into an Orc-infested dungeon containing the Sword of the Flaming Heart. Later expeditions involve increasingly varied opposition: Chaos servants, Skaven, magical guardians and Undead all appear over the course of the quest. The Wand becomes especially important for the Wizard; the Power Bow is tied to Elven magic; and the World’s Edge Hammer is a formidable Dwarven weapon that can be thrown and magically return to its wielder. By the time the Hammer is recovered, Felspraag is effectively becoming a war camp, with most able-bodied defenders committed to fighting the Lichemaster’s growing armies. Supplies, healing and recruits become scarce, emphasizing that time is running out.

The final stage takes place in the Caverns of the Lichemaster. Both dungeon levels are fully mapped and much more scripted than standard exploration. Once the Heroes are transported to the lower level, retreat is impossible: victory or death are the only exits. The Lichemaster’s forces include Undead, his vampire servant Orlock, the Chaos Lord Joachim Masreik, magically trapped Skaven, magical skulls and numerous special effects created by the Lichemaster’s increasing power.

The adventure is designed as an escalating epic. What starts as a quest for one lost sword becomes a race to reassemble the magical armament of a previous generation and finish a battle that those legendary Heroes could not. The separate expeditions also make character advancement and accumulated resources feel integral to the story: by the finale, the party is expected to have grown considerably since its arrival in Felspraag.

3. The Quest for Sonneklinge

The adventure begins in Carroburg, a town whose history is dominated by the legendary Hero Prince Karad von Carron. Centuries earlier Karad defeated a Goblinoid horde attacking the town, killing the Ogre Ratgot Dwarfmangler even though the dying Ogre mortally wounded him in return. Karad was buried in a tomb in the Skaag Hills together with Sonneklinge, the sword with which he killed Dwarfmangler. For generations the surrounding countryside remained comparatively peaceful. Recently, however, Orc attacks have resumed and become increasingly destructive.

Karad’s descendant, Erich von Carron, reveals the supernatural cause. Prince Karad’s ghost has appeared to him because Sonneklinge has been stolen from his tomb, leaving the old Hero’s spirit unable to rest. The Orcs responsible for the new attacks have also gained a dangerous new leader: the Ogre Chieftain Hogrod Trollslaughterer, descended from the same brutal line as Ratgot Dwarfmangler. Hogrod has displaced the former Orc Warlord Gragath Rogant and established himself among the Orc tribes occupying the abandoned Dwarven settlement of Kazrad-Kain.

The Heroes are asked to enter Kazrad-Kain, find Hogrod’s stronghold, recover Sonneklinge, and return the sword to Prince Karad’s tomb. The campaign therefore has two strongly contrasting parts. The first is an assault on an occupied underground stronghold filled with Orcs and their allies, culminating in the Ogre Chieftain’s domain. The emphasis is conventional heroic dungeon warfare: break through the enemy settlement, defeat its leaders and recover the stolen relic.

The second half shifts into a tomb adventure. Recovering Sonneklinge is not sufficient—the weapon must be restored to Karad’s resting place. The Heroes are specifically warned not to steal anything from the Prince’s tomb, because Karad placed a curse on the treasure buried with him. That warning matters because the tomb presents precisely the sort of valuable grave goods adventurers are normally conditioned to loot. The scenario therefore creates a deliberate moral and practical temptation: success means restoring what belongs to the dead, not stripping the burial chamber for profit.

The campaign’s central theme is the repetition of history. Karad died killing an Ogre ancestor; his stolen sword now falls into the orbit of another Ogre from the same monstrous lineage. The modern Heroes effectively complete Karad’s unfinished legacy by defeating the new threat and restoring his weapon. Compared with some of the larger campaigns in the book, Sonneklinge is relatively focused and traditional, but it has a strong sense of place, ancestral obligation and the consequences of disturbing the dead.

4. The Priests of Pleasure

This is an adventure for beginning Heroes built around a rescue mission rather than a search for a magical artefact. The Heroes are contacted through their merchant friend Klaus Krudenwald on behalf of another merchant, Eberhardt Kaldach. Eberhardt’s brother Wilhelm disappeared after his caravan was ambushed while travelling between Hovelhof and Bergsburg. His guards were slaughtered, Wilhelm vanished, and two dead attackers were found wearing the pastel-blue robes of a supposedly harmless local cult known as the Priests of Pleasure.

Eberhardt concludes that the cult has kidnapped Wilhelm for sacrifice. He offers the Heroes 1,000 Gold Crowns and magical items if Wilhelm is brought back alive, or half the reward if his body can at least be recovered. The Priests operate from a ruined temple outside Bergsburg, and what had previously appeared to be eccentric but harmless worship is revealed as a Chaos-tainted cult conducting sacrifices in exchange for supernatural power.

The Heroes descend through the cult’s dungeon toward its Sanctuary, where Wilhelm is being held. The opposition changes as they penetrate deeper. The monster matrices explicitly divide the inhabitants between corrupted human cultists—Chaos Thugs, Champions, Warriors and Sentries—and Beastmen. This makes the dungeon feel like an organized religious conspiracy gradually giving way to increasingly overt Chaos corruption.

A particularly important element is time. Wilhelm is due to be sacrificed when Morrslieb next becomes full. The quest can take more than one expedition, but every additional attempt increases the possibility that the Heroes will arrive too late. When a new expedition begins, a roll determines whether Wilhelm has already been killed. If so, his corpse is found in the sacrificial pit rather than alive in his cell. The Heroes can still retrieve his body for half payment, but carrying a corpse severely limits movement and action.

The journey inward and the journey out are intentionally different experiences. On the descent, communication among the cult’s ranks is poor, so the defence is comparatively fragmented. Once the Heroes have reached the Sanctuary, however, the leadership knows there is an intrusion. On the return to the surface, Dungeon Counters become significantly more likely, representing the cult gathering its forces and pursuing the rescuers. Thus the scenario’s climax is not necessarily the deepest room: escaping with Wilhelm may be more dangerous than reaching him.

The result is one of the collection’s clearest “in-and-out” adventures: infiltrate, locate the captive, then survive a fighting retreat. Its threat is not only combat but delay.

5. The Dark Beneath the World

The Dark Beneath the World is written for a significantly more experienced party than the introductory adventures. The book recommends four or five Heroes with roughly twenty completed quests between them, or alternatively provides powerful pregenerated characters based on Gotrek, Felix and their companions. Unlike many Advanced Heroquest campaigns, this one is explicitly a single expedition. Once the Heroes enter the undercity, they must finish the quest, die, or abandon it; leaving the undercity ends the adventure permanently.

That restriction changes the tone dramatically. There is no rhythm of scouting a level, retreating to town, purchasing supplies and returning stronger. Resources expended underground stay expended, wounds and losses matter immediately, and every decision about whether to explore further carries greater weight. The pre-generated party is considered powerful enough that it requires no Henchmen, although players using their own characters may bring as many as they wish. The adventure also incorporates an Amethyst Wizard spellbook for Johan when the provided characters are used.

The setting is an immense undercity, presented as a distinctive environment rather than a normal dungeon. The principal organised enemies are Goblinoids from the Snarling Sun and Howling Moon tribes. The monster tables also allow Orcs, Goblins, Skaven, Chaos creatures and other inhabitants to appear, suggesting a vast subterranean ecosystem rather than a single faction’s fortress. The scenario therefore emphasizes hostile territory on a larger scale: the Heroes are moving through a buried world occupied by rival monsters, not merely clearing a compact lair.

The contents divide the adventure into general exploration, Special Locations, and finally The Halls of the Ancestors. These scripted areas interrupt the random or semi-random undercity exploration and provide the important narrative encounters. The Halls form the adventure’s major destination and culmination, while the rules for leaving the undercity make the final emergence to the surface part of the quest’s resolution rather than merely an ordinary withdrawal.

One of the adventure’s defining qualities is therefore endurance. The party is expected to be veteran, well-equipped and capable of dealing with large groups of powerful enemies, but it cannot rely on repeated expeditions to grind down opposition. The undercity is meant to feel like a journey through an enormous hostile realm in which the Heroes become increasingly isolated from safety.

The book specifically recommends familiarity with the associated story material because much of the scenario’s appeal comes from the atmosphere of the undercity and from recreating the exploits of Gotrek, Felix and their companions. Even when original player characters are substituted, the adventure is built to preserve that same sense of entering a place far larger and more dangerous than an ordinary dungeon.

6. The Eyes of Chaos

This quest is intended for experienced Heroes, preferably a party that has already completed something like The Shattered Amulet. They are accompanied by the Bretonnian mercenary captain Pierre Chancier. The adventure begins at the Tombs of the Phalanx, an ancient cave complex in the Vault Mountains where Bretonnian soldiers killed in a great battle against Chaos were buried centuries earlier. Veterans still make an annual pilgrimage there to honour the dead. This year only one pilgrim returned, deliriously describing Ogres that behaved with extraordinary discipline and tactical intelligence. The Heroes are sent to clear the Ogres out.

The apparent Ogre problem hides a much more dangerous enemy. The Ogres are servants of Johannes Wiesehofer, a Chaos Sorcerer who murdered his own master, stole his magical books and treasures, and took possession of a strange Ogre Cloak. The cloak gives its wearer Ogre-like physical power and lets Johannes dominate Ogre servants. Following his stolen research, he found a powerful Chaos Flux beneath the tombs and began experimenting with it.

The upper level consists of the tomb complex itself. Pierre is personally invested in the mission because his fellow soldiers were slaughtered by the Ogres. He refuses payment and will not permit the Heroes to rob the Bretonnian tombs, although monster treasure is acceptable. If the adventure succeeds he can remain with the Warrior Hero as a loyal Henchman.

Below the tombs are two completely mapped dungeon levels containing Johannes’ servants and the adventure’s signature hazard: the Eyes of Chaos. These are small floating skull-like magical manifestations whose zones of influence create random Chaos effects. Depending on the Eye, magic may become difficult, magical items can be drained or temporarily nullified, armour can cease to protect the Heroes, enemy weapons can gain flaming power, or Fate Points can become unusable. The effects can reach around corners, meaning Heroes may enter a dangerous magical zone before they can even see its source.

Destroying an Eye is itself dangerous. They can only be harmed by magical attacks and they explode when destroyed, potentially injuring the Hero responsible. The lower levels also contain unusually disciplined monsters, some capable of opening doors and coordinating attacks far better than standard dungeon inhabitants.

The adventure therefore begins as an Ogre hunt and gradually reveals itself as a Chaos-magic nightmare. Its principal dramatic shift is the realization that the Ogres are merely guards and that the true enemy is a sorcerer exploiting raw Chaos beneath a sacred burial site.

7. The Trollslayer’s Oath

This quest takes the Heroes into the ruined Dwarven colony of Durrag-Dol in the World’s Edge Mountains. They are accompanied by the Trollslayer Kili Thekkrsson, who has sworn an oath to return the ancient warhammer Sognirstane, or “Roaring Stone,” to the Temple of the Living Ancestor within the colony. Kili asks for no wages; his price is the Heroes’ assistance in fulfilling the oath. He also forbids them to plunder Clan Durrag’s ancestral treasures. Skaven property, on the other hand, is fair game, and the Imperial Dwarf League Council pays a bounty for Skaven ears.

Durrag-Dol is not simply another cave complex. It is a failed Dwarven settlement dating from the ancient Elf-Dwarf wars. The colony was never completed because its earl-king, hearth guard and warriors were wiped out while campaigning away from home. Without defenders, the surviving settlement was later overrun by Orcs and Goblins. In the present day its ruins have become a Skaven nest.

The scenario makes the physical structure of a Dwarven hold central to play. The upper portions consist of farm galleries, followed by the Main Shaft, then the Gauntlet, and ultimately the Kinhearth. Workshops and mines lie still deeper but are flooded and inaccessible. Kili’s knowledge of traditional Dwarven construction is therefore crucial; unlike a normal dungeon where no one knows what lies ahead, the Heroes have a guide who understands the purpose and likely organization of the ancient spaces.

The opposition escalates as the party descends. Durrag-Dol is heavily infested by Skaven and their altered creatures, and the later levels contain powerful Skaven leaders. The special-character material identifies Shaffat as the nest’s nominal Warlord but notes that Tsekkaz is the true dominant figure. Tsekkaz bears the Black Hand of Death, a warpstone-studded enchanted gauntlet capable of inflicting devastating damage on Humans, Dwarfs and Elves.

The goal is not fundamentally to loot or conquer the colony. The quest succeeds by carrying Sognirstane through the corrupted ruins and restoring it to its proper shrine. That makes Kili’s oath the organizing principle of the entire adventure. The Heroes are visitors in a dead people’s sacred home, and the restrictions on looting reinforce the distinction between ancestral property and the possessions of the Skaven occupiers.

The scenario thus combines a deep multi-level descent with a strong cultural theme: reclaiming, however briefly, a desecrated Dwarven hold so that an ancient obligation can finally be discharged.

8. The Changing Faces of Tzeentch

The Heroes begin this adventure as bounty hunters pursuing Herman Zorin, an infamous outlaw of the Reikwald Forest. Zorin has murdered travellers for years, and hunters who tried to claim the enormous bounty on him met gruesome deaths. Then he suddenly vanished. After a month of following rumours, bribed road wardens and divinations, the Heroes trace him to the ruined Fortress of Veitch, a place with another ominous history: the Imperial Hero and witch hunter Alaric Von Loth disappeared there together with his legendary weapon, the White Reaver.

What the Heroes do not know is that Zorin is not merely a bandit. He is the last surviving master of the Bloody Mask, a cult devoted to Tzeentch. The Fortress of Veitch was the cult’s traditional base. Von Loth discovered the cult and attacked it, killing most of its masters but suffering a mortal wound. Zorin returned afterward and used the power of the cult’s artefacts—the Changing Faces of Tzeentch—to resurrect Von Loth as a servant of Chaos. He then filled the fortress with traps and began rebuilding the cult.

The adventure is divided into three dungeons, and the Heroes initially misunderstand their real objective. The first appears to be the hunt for Zorin, but its essential purpose is to recover Alaric Von Loth’s journal. The journal reveals the deeper secret of the fortress and explains how to reach the remaining areas. The second dungeon contains the magical key required to open the third. Thus progress is investigative as well as martial: each layer reveals that the apparent outlaw hunt is actually an assault on a surviving Chaos cult.

The changing masks are the source of the cult’s supernatural power, and the scenario increasingly shifts from outlaw hideout to Tzeentchian stronghold. Chaos characters can move through the dungeons, and the fortress contains multiple special adversaries rather than a single stationary boss. The surviving cultists, magical defences and trap network all reinforce Zorin’s determination never to suffer the kind of surprise attack that destroyed the previous masters.

The presence of Von Loth adds a grim inversion to the scenario. The legendary Hero who once nearly eradicated the cult has himself been turned into one of its weapons. The Heroes therefore confront not only Zorin and the artefacts but the corrupted legacy of the man who came closest to defeating them.

At heart, The Changing Faces of Tzeentch is a revelation-driven adventure: outlaw hunt, hidden cult, recovered journal, magical key, sealed inner dungeon and finally the source of the corruption.

9. Rivers of Blood

This adventure is designed for experienced Heroes and begins with what sounds like a classic haunted-house commission. Johannes von Bleistift has inherited Alptraum Manor after the death of his uncle, Count Rutger. Since the burial, Johannes claims to be plagued by nightmares and unexplained noises from the cellars and catacombs beneath the house. He hires the Heroes to investigate.

When they arrive, Johannes presents himself as a nervous, rather ignorant young nobleman who has supposedly spent years away in Marienburg and knows little about the manor’s underground passages. He leads the Heroes to a trapdoor descending into the cellars. His story is largely a lie.

Count Rutger is a Vampire. He is physically dead but continues to exist beneath the manor as a powerful Undead creature. Ordinary victims are no longer sufficient to sustain him; he requires the blood of exceptional people. Johannes knows this perfectly well. He serves his uncle in the hope that Rutger will eventually reward him with the blood kiss and transform him into a Vampire as well. Hiring the Heroes is therefore an elaborate feeding scheme: Johannes deliberately sends powerful adventurers underground so Rutger can hunt them.

As soon as the Heroes enter the underground complex, the situation becomes a survival scenario. Alptraum Manor contains two levels of cellars and catacombs, swarming with Undead servants. Rutger also prepared the place even before death by employing a master locksmith and thief to trap many of its doors, so the environment itself is hostile. The Heroes soon discover that they are trapped below and cannot simply return by the route they entered. Escape requires confronting or incapacitating the Vampire.

One location offers an important refuge: a tomb on the upper level consecrated by a priest of Morr, the God of Death. Rutger cannot enter it, and the Heroes can use it as a sanctuary to rest and recover Fate Points. Finding this protected chamber dramatically improves their chances of surviving the rest of the dungeon.

Rutger is intended to be more than a conventional boss waiting at the end of a corridor. The GM notes describe him as a special character designed specifically to frustrate and infuriate the party. That gives the adventure a predatory quality: the Heroes have entered the Vampire’s domain, and he is hunting them as much as they are searching for him.

Rivers of Blood is therefore a deliberate reversal of the normal dungeon premise. The Heroes think they have accepted a paid investigation. In reality they are the treasure placed inside the dungeon for the monster to collect.

10. Tobaro’s Bane

Tobaro’s Bane is the most direct and urgent adventure in the collection. The Tilean city of Tobaro is being overwhelmed by Skaven. A nearby clan has risen dramatically in strength under the leadership of the powerful Skaven Sorcerer Cankerclaw Darkheart, and the Ratmen have flooded out of the sewers into the city streets. Citizens are effectively trapped indoors, the city government is desperate, and the City Fathers call for outside Heroes to destroy the source of the invasion.

The objective is simple: find and kill Cankerclaw. The City Fathers provide the four Heroes with four Men-at-Arms and a Sergeant at no cost. The party must descend through a three-level Skaven dungeon, fight its way to the Sorcerer and return to the surface. If Cankerclaw is killed, the organized attacks on Tobaro are broken and the Heroes win.

The critical restriction is that the Heroes have only one expedition. Tobaro does not have time for them to scout the dungeon, withdraw, heal, buy equipment and try again. If they retreat without completing the mission, the opportunity is lost. Because of this all-or-nothing structure, success awards additional Fate Points.

The dungeon itself is generated largely using the normal exploration rules. Each level contains only one true Quest Room. On the first and second levels that room provides the stairs downward; later Quest Room results become ordinary Lairs. The composition of the enemy force draws on the Skaven tables from The Shattered Amulet, so the Heroes face familiar Clan warriors and specialist characters but under much greater time pressure.

The opposition grows stronger with depth. The first level includes an Assassin. The second brings Clan Pestilens and Skryre threats such as a Plague Monk and Warpweaver. The third combines a Plague Censer Bearer, Warlord and Sorcerer, with Cankerclaw as the ultimate target. Several of these enemies possess magical equipment, though some specifically Skaven items cannot be used by Heroes.

Another difference from an ordinary dungeon is persistence. If the Heroes escape from a Skaven group without killing it, the survivors return to their original location and attack again if the party passes through. Running away therefore does not clear the route for later.

Tobaro’s Bane strips Advanced Heroquest down to a concentrated pressure-cooker scenario: one party, one descent, one chance to kill the enemy leader before the city falls. It is less about long-term campaign planning than endurance, resource management and surviving a steadily escalating Skaven gauntlet.
